AI Implementation

AI is a tool for a specific task, not a strategy on its own.

We treat AI implementation the same way we treat any other change to a business: find where it actually helps first, then build only what earns its place. The work runs in the same order the rest of the consulting does.

1. Where AI fits — found during the assessment

An AI Implementation project does not start with a tool. It starts with the Vantage Point Review, where we look at how the business actually runs today: what takes the owner's or the team's time, where the same judgment gets made over and over, and where a delay or a bottleneck costs money or attention.

Some of what we find has nothing to do with AI — a staffing gap, a pricing problem, a missing process. Some of it does. We name those places specifically, in plain language, before any tool is chosen.

2. Putting it in — a scoped project

Once we know where AI belongs, the work becomes a defined project with a start and an end, not an open-ended engagement. A project typically includes:

Scope

The specific task or workflow being changed, written down and agreed before work starts.

Tools chosen

Selecting an existing AI tool where one fits well, or specifying a purpose-built one where it does not. No vendor is chosen in advance of the need.

Setup

Connecting the tool to how the business actually works, with the data and access it genuinely needs and nothing more.

Staff training

Showing the people who will use it day to day how it works, what it is good at, and where to still use their own judgment.

Handover

A working tool the business owns and can run without us, with plain documentation of what it does and how to change it.

3. Keeping it useful — the dashboard and ongoing advice

A tool that works on day one can drift out of date. Keeping AI useful is ongoing, not a one-time install: checking that it is still doing what it was built to do, adjusting it as the business changes, and turning it off if it stops earning its place.

That ongoing view is meant to live in the owner's dashboard, alongside the rest of the business.

What your dashboard shows

The dashboard has not been built. Nothing on this website provides it today. The sample Review shows the map, findings, snapshots, what-if levers and monthly trend on an invented business; the AI-tools view below is not in it. This section describes what it is planned to show once it exists, so the plan can be reviewed before anything is built. We would build and keep it up to date; you would see the finished view.

As we picture it, an owner signing in would see a plain map of the business, not a sales pitch, built from the Vantage Point Review:

  • A map of how the business actually works and where the money flows — work and materials going out to customers, money coming back, and where it splits: staff, suppliers, overhead, tax, owner pay and profit.
  • Findings from the review grouped into Fix Now, Improve Next and Build Toward, each colored by which of the four lenses it belongs to — financial, operational, owner & organizational, or transferability & exit readiness.
  • Before-and-after snapshots showing what has changed since the last review.
  • What-if levers you can move to see the arithmetic behind a change — clearly labeled as simple math on your own numbers, not a forecast or a prediction.
  • A monthly trend strip showing the business's direction over time.
  • Any AI tools currently in use in the business, in plain terms, and what effect each one is having on the task it was built for.

Every figure the dashboard shows would come from real work already done with you — an assessment, a project, an advisory conversation — never invented or estimated to look complete. Where something has not happened yet, the dashboard would say so plainly instead of showing a blank chart or a made-up number.

What this is not

We do not sell AI tools, and we do not recommend a tool because of who makes it. We do not promise a result, a cost saving or a timeline before a project is scoped, and we do not provide legal, tax, securities or investment advice.
