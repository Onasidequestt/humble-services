A business can make money and still be difficult to own.

Many established businesses are profitable but demanding. Decisions still run through the owner. Systems live in one person's head. A key customer or employee carries more weight than anyone would like. And the next chapter, whether stepping back, handing over to family or a manager, or selling, feels hard to plan for.

If your business is at a crossroads, you do not need a louder growth plan. You need a clear look at the whole company.

What happens to your business without you?

Explore the Humble Business 360 Assessment

Four lenses on the whole business

We look at the numbers, the systems, the people, the owner, the risks and the future, together.

In a full assessment these four lenses expand into six assessment areas.

Financial Health

How the business actually makes money: revenue quality, margins, pricing, labor costs, cash flow and reporting.

Operational Health

Staffing, responsibilities, workflow, systems, documentation and bottlenecks. Could it run for 90 days without you?

Owner & Organizational Health

Your role, workload, delegation, priorities and what you want the business to become. Advisory work, not therapy.

Transferability & Exit Readiness

Whether someone other than you could own and run the company well, and what has to change first.

The founder

Clark Patrick is an entrepreneur, business owner, operator, and founder of Humble Services Business Consulting.

Over the course of his career, he has founded and operated multiple businesses, participated in start-ups and entrepreneurial ventures, personally hired, supervised, or managed more than 100 employees, negotiated extensive client and vendor contracts, navigated complex business litigation, and transitioned out of businesses he founded or owned. Today, he owns Humble Gardens in Hawaiʻi along with other business ventures.

Through Humble Services Business Consulting, Clark works with business owners who want practical help strengthening their operations, improving systems, preparing for transition, and better understanding the businesses they have spent years building.
