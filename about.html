About Humble Services

How we look at a business

An accountant sees the books and an attorney sees the contracts. We look at the whole company together: the numbers, the systems, the people, the owner, the risks and the future.

The founder's story

Clark Patrick
Founder, Owner and CEO, Humble Services Business Consulting

Clark is an entrepreneur, business owner, operator, and founder of Humble Services Business Consulting. Over the course of his career, he has built, managed, partnered in, transitioned out of, and advised businesses across a wide range of industries and stages of development.

His experience has been shaped less by theory than by the realities of running businesses: hiring and managing people, negotiating contracts, resolving disputes, managing cash flow, working with clients, building brands, developing systems, navigating legal challenges, and making difficult decisions when circumstances change.

He has founded and operated multiple businesses and has been involved in several start-ups and entrepreneurial ventures. Over the years, he has personally hired, supervised, or managed more than 100 employees, negotiated and administered extensive client and vendor contracts, transitioned out of businesses he founded or owned, and dealt firsthand with complex, high-level business litigation and legal disputes. He has also bought and sold real estate for personal and business ventures, and has experience evaluating assets, negotiating transactions and structuring business relationships.

Today, Clark owns Humble Gardens in Hawaiʻi, along with other business ventures. Running service businesses and entrepreneurial ventures has given him particularly deep experience with many of the issues faced by small-business owners: employees, pricing, profitability, customer relationships, contracts, scheduling, operational efficiency, owner dependence, succession planning, and the challenge of building a business that can function effectively beyond the founder's day-to-day involvement.

Earlier in his career, Clark worked as a commercial photographer and director in Minneapolis, producing lifestyle, aerial, portrait, conceptual, and motion campaigns for companies, advertising agencies, and design firms. That experience developed a complementary set of skills in branding, visual communication, marketing, creative direction, and understanding how businesses present themselves to the outside world. He is also a published writer.

Clark is a private pilot and has owned, bought, and sold several airplanes. Aviation reinforced many of the principles that also shape his approach to business: preparation matters, systems matter, small problems are easier to address before they become large ones, and good judgment is often more valuable than unnecessary complexity.

Humble Services Business Consulting grew out of Clark's belief that many business owners — particularly founders who have spent decades building successful companies — do not necessarily need abstract consulting theory. They need someone who understands what ownership actually feels like and can help them organize what they have built, identify problems and opportunities, improve how the business operates, and prepare for whatever comes next.

His approach is practical, direct, and collaborative. The objective is not to impose a predetermined formula on a company, but to understand the owner, the business, its history, its financial and operational realities, and the goals of the people involved.

Outside of business, Clark's interests include aviation, gardening and agriculture, writing, design, stand-up paddleboarding, and spending time outdoors. He lives on the Big Island of Hawaiʻi and is the father of a four-year-old daughter, who remains the most important part of his life.

What this work is, and what it is not

We offer practical business advisory and decision support. That includes the human side of ownership: workload, delegation, conflict, difficult employees or customers, and what you want the business to become.

Owner advisory is not psychotherapy or mental-health treatment. We are not a law firm, an accounting or tax firm, a business broker, a valuation firm or an investment adviser. When a question needs one of those professionals, we say so and help you prepare for that conversation.†

What we believe

  • Owner-led businesses matter. Companies with a handful of people up to about forty are where owners, employees, customers and families meet most directly. When one of these businesses is healthy, it supports many lives. When it depends entirely on one person, all of that is at risk.
  • Transition planning starts years early. Clean financials, documented systems, a capable team and a spread of customers take time to build. Starting early gives an owner choices, instead of letting the market decide the value for them.
  • More revenue is not always a better business. A bigger company can be a harder one to own. The goal is a business that is financially healthy, operationally sound, personally sustainable for the owner, and able to succeed without permanent dependence on that owner.
  • A good business should work financially, operationally and personally.
  • Straight answers beat jargon.
  • Owners deserve an outside view before a big decision, not after.
  • Recommend more work only when it is genuinely warranted.
