Bookkeeping and Hosting Map

How your CPA, CFO and accountants would log in to the books, and where the whole platform should live as Humble Services grows.

This is a proposal for you to check. We build it once you are happy with it. The sign-in rules, the platform and the wall around client records already work in our test version. The bookkeeping, the back-ups and the hosts are not set up yet.

The map

Read it from the top down: the people, the web address they type, the sign-in, the platform, and the data. Each shaded area is where we recommend that part lives.

Bookkeeping: each person works inside the platform

Your CPA, CFO and accountants each get their own secure login and do their work in the platform itself. Downloads stay available for back-up. Here is how we suggest the work splits; tell us if your team divides it differently.

CFO

Sees all of the books and every report. Signs off each month once it is complete.

Your CPA

Sees the books, makes year-end and tax adjustments, and locks a month once it is closed.

Accountants

The day-to-day work: record entries, match payments to invoices, and send invoices.

  • Every change carries the name of the person who made it, and when.
  • Nothing is erased. A mistake is fixed with a correcting entry, so the history stays whole.
  • A locked month cannot be changed.
  • You invite each person, and you can remove anyone's login at any time.
  • The books team sees the money side only. Your clients' business files and call notes stay open to you only.

Our hosting recommendation

Keep Bluehost for what it does well, and put the platform with two services built for this kind of work. Both are independently audited for security (SOC 2 Type 2). Supabase shares its audit report on its Team plan, which we suggest once real client data goes in.

Bluehost: your address and website

Keeps humblebusinessconsulting.com, the public website, your other websites and, once set up, your email. Nothing you have there changes.

Render: runs the platform

Keeps the platform running day and night, including the sign-in and the AI tools. It can grow to bigger machines as the company grows.

Supabase: keeps the data

The platform's data is stored in PostgreSQL, a standard database that very large companies use. We wrote its access rules to work on Supabase from the start, and the logins live in the same database, in their own separate area.

Why not run the platform on Bluehost?

Bluehost's shared plans can't keep a program like the platform running all the time. Its larger private servers can, but then Humble Services would be running its own server: security updates, back-ups and outages would all be yours. For your clients' financial records, we would rather leave that to services whose whole job it is.

If Humble Services grows very large

Render and Supabase both grow by plan: bigger machines, and extra read-only copies of the database for heavy use. If the company ever outgrows them, the data is standard PostgreSQL, so a complete copy, logins included, can move to a very large cloud such as Amazon's. That later step would take some rework, which we would plan well ahead. Your data is never locked in.

How the move works

Nothing real has to be moved: no client data goes into the platform until it runs on the new hosts. So the move is a fresh set-up, checked before anyone uses it.

  1. Finish hereEvery launch check passes on our test version, using test data only.
  2. You open the accountsRender and Supabase, in the name of Humble Services, Inc. You own them and invite us in.
  3. Set up and re-checkWe set the platform up there and run every launch check again, on the new hosts.
  4. Point the addressOne address setting sends the app address to the platform. The website and email stay as they are.
  5. Invite your teamYou first, then your CPA, CFO and accountants. Real client data goes in only after that.
